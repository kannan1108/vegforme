The moment

The food system
never asked for your consent.

It was built before you were born. The factory. The feedlot. The processing plant. The marketing that made it all feel normal. You inherited it.

Walking away is not a grand gesture. It is a series of small ones — a label read, a question asked, a habit interrupted. The door is always right there. Most people just never look at it directly.

I looked. This is what I found on the other side.

The connection

Your plate and
the planet are
the same conversation.

The deforestation in Brazil is connected to the beef on a California menu. The antibiotic resistance crisis is connected to the chicken sandwich. The aquifer depletion in the Central Valley is connected to the almond milk argument.

None of this requires guilt. Guilt is paralytic. What it requires is curiosity — a willingness to follow the thread from plate to consequence and decide, consciously, what you want your part in it to be.

I spent decades eating whatever was in front of me. Then I lost 26 pounds eating plants and realized the weight was never the point. The attention was the point.

When you start paying attention to what you eat, you cannot stop paying attention. The question of where your food came from leads to the question of what it cost to get here. Which leads to the question of who — or what — paid that cost.

I grew up eating meat. I ate it without thinking, which is exactly how the system needs you to eat it. The moment you start thinking, the whole architecture becomes visible. The feedlot. The slaughterhouse. The supply chain engineered to place as much distance as possible between the animal and the plate.

I am not a vegetarian. I still have work to do. But I eat differently than I did — with more knowledge, more intention, and more awareness of the golden thread in that image above. The one that connects me, whether I acknowledge it or not, to every living thing my choices touch.

Help Ever. Hurt Never. Four words I've carried for twenty years as a spiritual principle. I am only now understanding they apply at the dinner table too.
